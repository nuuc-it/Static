Mark up a score

How to make a score follow the music

A score follows the music because of marks: points on the page, each tied to a time in the recording. Editing is only needed when a score has no marks or they need fixing. Saving for everyone needs a signed-in editor; anyone else's edits stay on their own device.

The score has two modes: Play (listen and follow) and Edit, which has two parts, Layout (place and label marks) and Sync (time marks to the music). Lay out every line first, then time the marks.

Start editing

  1. Open the score (music-note button), tap the gear and choose Edit - Layout or Edit - Sync under Mode. Play shows no edit controls.
  2. In Edit the header has Done (back to Play), a Layout | Sync switch, the gear and close.
  3. Under the header, Undo steps back. Save appears for editors. More holds Revert to saved, Clear all, Set track offset and the Reaction slider.
  4. Zoom is by pinch (on a computer, ctrl or cmd and the wheel).

Layout: add marks

Layout shows the page view with no transport and no times. The chip says what a tap adds: Mark, Jump or Note. Tap empty score to add one.

  1. Mark: a point where a line or bar starts. It is placed into the nearest line, or starts a new line with its own box. It has no time until you time it in Sync.
  2. Note: a text note. Singers see it when they tap it.
  3. Jump numbers: a jump is numbered in the mark list like a mark, in reading order (page, line, left to right). A jump between #3 and #4 is #4 and the old #4 becomes #5. Notes and end-of-line marks have no number.
  4. Jump: a repeat or skip. It points to a mark; in Play, tapping it goes to that mark. A jump can point to a mark that has no time yet; it plays once that mark is timed. After you place a jump, tap the mark it goes to (or press Cancel and choose it in Goes to). A jump that goes back is a Repeat sign; one that goes forward is a Jump.
  5. While a mark is selected, the first tap on empty score only closes its sheet; the next tap adds.

The line box

The first mark of each line carries a box: the part of the page the sideways Ribbon shows for that line. Select a line's first mark in Layout and its box appears with a handle on each corner and side.

  1. Drag a handle to resize the box, or drag inside it (away from a mark) to move it. A box cannot leave the page or cut out its own marks. Each drag is one Undo step. A drag never adds a mark; wait a moment after a drag before you tap to add one.
  2. Dragging a mark moves only the mark, and it stops at the edge of its box.
  3. The S, A, T and B circles beside the box mark where each voice part sits in that line. Drag one up or down to put it on its staff; it snaps to another circle or the line when within half an inch (hold Shift to turn the snap off). A new line's box starts with the circles in the same places as the last box you changed.
  4. A new line starts with the size of the previous line's box. Select a mark and tap Size all boxes to give every line the selected box's width and height; each line keeps its top-left corner. One Undo step.
  5. Tap inside a line's box to add a mark to that line; it lines up with the first mark. A tap on a handle adds nothing.
  6. Every line shows on the Ribbon, timed or not. A line with no times is crossed at the speed of the lines around it.

The mark editor

In Layout, tap a mark to select it. A sheet at the bottom shows its fields. Tap empty score to close it.

  1. Label: a name such as 12. Each label can be used once. A jump has a label and a target.
  2. Goes to, Passes and Endings: Goes to lists every mark, timed or not; Tap on page lets you tap the mark on the score instead. On a repeat sign, Passes (- and +) is the total number of passes, 2 or more. + Add endings takes two taps: where the 1st ending starts (inside the repeat), then the first mark of the 2nd ending (after the repeat sign; tap empty score to add a mark there). A banner above the score guides each tap, and Cancel or Escape writes nothing. The Endings row then reads 1. at #4 → 2. #5, with Select 1. and Remove. Changing Passes moves the 1st ending with it. On a forward jump, Taken chooses Every time, From pass N or Only pass N, and Show as ending (from pass 2 on) draws 1. and 2. None of this needs audio or times.
  3. Pass and repeats: A mark inside a repeat shows a Pass choice: Set to now and Clear time act on the pass you pick (Pass 1 is the original mark). While the playhead is inside a repeat, a thin bar in the pass's colour sits above and below the playhead bar. A jump forward that is set as an ending shows 1. (or 1.2.) instead of the jump pill, and the mark it goes to shows 2..
  4. Pass times: after you time a jump back, Apply default times (N) (greyed out, with the reason, until the repeat sign and its target are timed) gives each unapplied pass mark its default time (not the mark the repeat sign lands on: its time is the repeat sign's own time, so timing one is timing the other) (the original time plus the repeat's shift); it never changes a verified time. In the NOW list an unapplied pass time is in brackets, an applied one starts with ~ and is italic, and a verified one is plain. Pick a pass mark and press Verify to accept its time, or use Set to now, which writes and verifies. There is no accept-all. In Sync, an untimed repeat sign is in the timing queue (it needs a time, because its time gives the defaults); a jump that is taken by pass needs none. The timing button goes on to the unapplied and unverified pass marks after the marks that need a time. A pass time that is not after the entry played before it is flagged.
  5. Jumps in the NOW list: each jump that is taken has one row with the time it happens, such as #22 Repeat :| -> #19.2 m54.2, and the row right after it starts with an arrow (-> #19.2 m54.2) to show where the jump lands; a later pass reads #22.2 ... -> #19.3. A jump that is reached on a pass where it is not taken (for example a 1st ending that only applies on pass 2) keeps its row there, greyed, with the reason beside it (#8 1st ending -> #10 (pass 2)); on the pass where it fires it has its own row (#8.2 1st ending -> #10) after the last mark before it. A jump that is never taken keeps one row with a flag that shows what is wrong: ! no time (a repeat sign needs a time: time it in Sync), ! no target (pick where it goes) or ! never taken (a forward jump with no time and no pass is never taken). Choosing a jump row selects the jump and puts the playhead 0.1 s before it, or, if the jump has no time, on the time of the timed mark just before it (Layout only selects).
  6. Timing a repeat: in Sync, while the second pass plays, Set to now times the repeated mark (m20.2), not the first one. To retime or clear one pass, pick its row in the NOW list (m20.2) and use Clear time to put it back to its default.
  7. Type: Mark, End of line, Jump or Note.
  8. Note: text shown when a singer taps the mark.
  9. Size all boxes, Clear time and Delete.

Sync: time the marks

Sync uses the Ribbon or the page view (the Ribbon setting) and has the transport: -5, -1, Play, +1, +5, speed, Loop and Follow. Nothing drags in Sync; a tap selects a mark, and a tap on empty score clears the selection.

  1. The timing button has one target. It is the selected mark; otherwise the first mark that needs a time or a fix, in reading order; otherwise, when every mark is timed, the first mark after the playhead. It reads TIME, FIX or RETIME with the mark's number. Tap it as the music reaches that mark: it sets the time to now (less the Reaction allowance while playing) and moves to the next target. It never moves the mark.
  2. -0.1 and +0.1 beside the button nudge the mark just set. Skip moves to the next target without a time.
  3. Needs time marks are hollow with a flag. Out of order marks are red with a ! and a struck-through time: times must increase in reading order. Play and Follow ignore out-of-order marks until they are fixed. The chip reads, for example, 5 need time . 2 out of order; tap it to go to the first one.
  4. The fix window is the span of time the target may take, between the marks either side of it. It shows as a band on the scrub bar with its times. A tap outside it is refused with a message and changes nothing. Loop window repeats the window.
  5. Pre-roll: when the playhead is already past the window, playback jumps to 3 s before it; otherwise it carries on.
  6. Estimate: for a mark with no time, a dashed line on the scrub bar shows where its time probably is, with a label such as 1:02. It never sets the time.
  7. Pinned: the view stays on the target. Tap the Pinned pill or Follow to go back to following the music.
  8. PREV / NOW / NEXT show the marks either side of the playhead. Tap the time in the middle to pick any timed mark from a list.
  9. In Layout the same strip shows no times. NOW names the mark you selected (or the one nearest the middle of the page); tap it for the list of every mark in the order it is played, with repeat passes (#2.2) and a row for each jump taken (:| -> #2). Choosing a row scrolls to it and selects it with that pass picked; it never moves the music. PREV and NEXT step through the same list.
  10. A selected mark shows its label and time with More: Go to (3 s before the mark), Clear time and Cursor here.
  11. Return (|◀): in Play, left of -5s, it goes back to exactly where you last pressed Play.

Save, Revert and Keep mine

  1. Save sends your marks to Drive for every singer. Only editors see it. It can be saved with marks still flagged; its tooltip gives the counts.
  2. Anyone whose copy differs from the saved score sees, in Play while paused, N differences from the saved score with Revert (and Save for an editor).
  3. If someone saved a newer version first, Save is refused. Tap Revert to take the saved marks, or Keep mine to save yours over them.
  4. Scrolling the Ribbon up or down does not count as a change; it is saved along with your next Save.

Set track offset

Each voice sits at a different height below the marks. Open More and tap Set track offset, choose the track, then tap where that voice's staff is under a line. Follow then keeps your focus track's staff in view. Starting playback turns the tool off.
